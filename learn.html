    What inspection data can and can't tell you

    Inspection results are useful, but they're easy to over-read. A few things to keep in mind:

    • An inspection is one visit. What the inspector finds depends on what's happening that day, how long they stay, and their judgment. No violations doesn't prove a kitchen is always safe.
    • Places with more complicated food get inspected more often than places that mostly sell packaged food, so you can't compare raw counts between them.
    • Follow-up inspections go back to places that already had problems, so repeat violations will look more common there.
    • Risk factor violations are the ones CDC links to outbreaks, which is why they come first on the form.
    • Some things never show up in this data: illnesses that weren't reported, complaints that didn't lead to an inspection, and problems fixed while the inspector was there.

    Words you'll see on reports

    TCS food (time/temperature control for safety)
    Food that needs time or temperature control to stop germs from growing or making toxins: meat, dairy, cooked rice and beans, cut tomatoes, cut leafy greens, cut melons and similar foods.
    Ready-to-eat (RTE) food
    Food that will be eaten without further cooking, so there is no step left to kill germs.
    Danger zone
    41°F to 135°F, the range where bacteria grow fastest. Cold TCS food is held at 41°F or below and hot TCS food at 135°F or above.
    PIC (person in charge)
    The person responsible for the operation during the visit.
    CFPM (Certified Food Protection Manager)
    Someone who has passed an accredited manager exam. The Food Code requires the person in charge to be one.
    Risk factor and public health intervention items
    Items 1 to 29 on the report. Practices most often linked to foodborne illness, and the controls that prevent them.
    Good retail practices
    Items 30 to 56. Basic sanitation, equipment and building upkeep. Called "Upkeep" on this site.
    Items 57 and 58
    Illinois additions to the FDA model form: food handler training (57) and allergen awareness training for certified managers (58).
    Repeat
    A violation that was also found at an earlier inspection. The county report counts repeats per visit; some inspectors also write "repeat" in their notes.
    Re-inspection
    A follow-up visit to check that problems from an earlier inspection were fixed. Called "Follow-up" on this site.
    Priority, Priority Foundation and Core
    How the FDA Food Code ranks each rule. Priority (P) rules directly prevent or reduce hazards, such as cooking temperatures. Priority Foundation (Pf) rules support them, such as having a thermometer. Core (C) rules cover general sanitation and upkeep.
    Date marking
    A label on ready-to-eat TCS food kept in the fridge more than 24 hours, showing when it must be used or thrown out (no more than 7 days at 41°F or below).
    Food Code citation
    The rule number the inspector cites, for example 3-501.17(A). Illinois uses the 2022 FDA Food Code.
