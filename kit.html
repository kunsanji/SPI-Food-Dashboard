Instructor plan

Learning objectives. By the end, students can (1) name the five CDC foodborne illness risk factors and link each to items on the Illinois inspection report, (2) tell risk factor findings from good retail practice findings in a real report, and (3) explain two limits of inspection data as a surveillance source.

Discussion prompts

  • A place has 8 upkeep findings and 0 risk factor findings. Another has 1 repeat risk factor finding. Which would worry you more, and why?
  • Why might a busy restaurant inspected four times a year show more findings than a convenience store inspected once?
  • Many jurisdictions post letter grades. Sangamon County does not. What are the trade-offs for the public, for businesses and for inspectors?
  • CDC estimates most foodborne illness is never linked to a specific cause. How should that shape how much weight we put on inspection findings?

Answer notes

Risk factor items (1 to 29) are those most linked to outbreaks; a repeat risk factor finding suggests a control that is not working over time. Inspection frequency, menu complexity and inspector judgment all affect counts, so raw comparisons across places mislead. Grades are easy to read but compress a snapshot into a label that can outlive the conditions it describes.
